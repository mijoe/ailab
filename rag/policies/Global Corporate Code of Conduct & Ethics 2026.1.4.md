# Policy: Global Corporate Code of Conduct & Ethics
**Version:** 2026.1.4 | **Status:** Active | **Owner:** Office of General Counsel

## 1. Introduction to Ethical Conduct
The organization's reputation is built on the integrity of its employees. This policy outlines the expectations for professional behavior when dealing with external entities, particularly third-party vendors, partners, and government officials.

## 4. Interaction with Third-Party Entities
### 4.1 Gifts, Hospitality, and Anti-Bribery
To maintain an objective procurement process, employees must strictly adhere to the "Bright Line" rule regarding vendor gifts.
* **Nominal Value:** Only items of "De Minimis" value (e.g., pens, calendars, or modest meals under $50) may be accepted without disclosure.
* **Prohibited Items:** Cash, gift cards, travel expenses, and high-value electronics are strictly prohibited.
* **Reporting Requirement:** Any gift exceeding $50, or any series of gifts from the same vendor totaling $150 in a calendar year, must be logged in the *Global Gift Registry* within 48 hours of receipt.
* **FCPA Compliance:** Under the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act, providing anything of value to a foreign official to secure a business advantage is a criminal offense and will result in immediate termination.

### 4.2 Conflict of Interest in Vendor Selection
Employees involved in the "Request for Proposal" (RFP) process must sign a Conflict of Interest (COI) waiver for every new project.
* **Family Ties:** If an employee's immediate family member is employed by a bidding vendor, that employee must recuse themselves from the evaluation committee.
* **Pre-existing Ownership:** Holding more than 1% equity in a third-party vendor must be disclosed to the Ethics Committee.

### 4.3 Ethics of "Shadow IT"
Acquiring third-party SaaS tools using personal or departmental credit cards without GISO approval is considered an ethical violation. While intended to increase productivity, "Shadow IT" bypasses the security vetting defined in EISP v1.1.0 and puts organizational data at risk.
